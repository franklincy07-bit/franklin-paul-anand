# Publishing checklist: Franklin Paul Anand

Run through this before publishing any article, video or lesson
(that is, before changing `draft: true` to `draft: false`).

## 1. Confidentiality

- [ ] No internal documents, unpublished data or internal performance figures.
- [ ] No donor, funder or programme names, budgets or contract details,
      unless they are already public **and** you have permission.
- [ ] Nothing learned in confidence (staff matters, negotiations, audits).

## 2. People

- [ ] No one is named, quoted or shown without their permission.
- [ ] Colleagues, partners, young people and communities are anonymised
      ("a district team", "a youth-led network").
- [ ] Extra care for young people: no identifiable photos or stories of
      minors without guardian consent.

## 3. Employers (current and past)

- [ ] Written in your own voice, not on behalf of any organisation.
- [ ] Nothing that could embarrass, misrepresent or compete with a current
      or former employer.
- [ ] Consistent with your current employment terms (outside work,
      publishing and social media policies).

## 4. Facts

- [ ] Every figure, date and claim is accurate and can be shared publicly.
- [ ] Achievements, awards and credentials are real and described exactly.
- [ ] Sources are credited where you use other people's work or data.

## 5. Images and video

- [ ] You own the photo or video, or have permission to use it.
- [ ] No third-party logos, branded clothing or faces without consent.
- [ ] Photos have no hidden location data. The site's build doesn't add
      any, but check original files from phones.

## 6. Final read

- [ ] The title and summary say what the piece offers the reader.
- [ ] Topics and type (`field-notes`, `insight`, `how-to`) are set.
- [ ] You'd be comfortable with your current employer, a future employer
      and the people in the story reading it.
