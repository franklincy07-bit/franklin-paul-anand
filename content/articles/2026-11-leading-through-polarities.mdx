_Part 3 of 5 in a series of my reflections, drawing on Gagan Sethi's "Broader Sectoral Perspective" module (Alliance for Change) on the FMSF Leadership Development Programme, February 2026._

Every leader carries a list of problems that never quite get fixed. Should we grow, or go deeper? Follow the donor's priorities, or our communities'? Keep the founder's vision, or share leadership more widely?

One of the most freeing ideas I have come across is that **many of these are not problems at all.** They are polarities.

## A problem has a solution. A polarity doesn't.

A polarity is a pair of seemingly opposite but equally necessary truths – two values that need each other over time. You cannot pick one and drop the other, and you cannot solve the tension once and for all.

<Highlight>A problem has one right answer. A polarity has <em>two right answers that need each other</em> – and a leader's job is to hold both.</Highlight>

<Pendulum caption="Without a map, teams swing from one side to the other – and pay for the downside of each. The healthy range is in the middle, holding both." />

## The polarities we live with

These are the tensions I see civil society leaders negotiating every day: **global vs local priorities, scale vs depth, advocacy vs compliance, founder vision vs collective leadership, short-term survival vs long-term transformation,** and **tradition vs innovation.**

The same pattern runs through organisations – centralisation and decentralisation, bureaucracy and entrepreneurship, quality and innovation – and through young people's lives, from respecting elders to questioning authority.

## Reflection keeps you steady

Holding a polarity depends on a habit of reflection. Drawing on Dewey and Schön, it helps to separate **reflection-in-action** (noticing and adjusting while something happens), **reflection-on-action** (looking back afterwards) and **reflection-for-action** (thinking ahead).

The simplest tool is Rolfe's three questions: **What?** What happened? **So what?** What does it tell us? **Now what?** What will we do differently? Fifteen minutes with your team after a hard week turns frustration into learning – and makes a polarity visible before it becomes a crisis.

## Questions worth asking

**For yourself**

- Which "problem" have you been trying to solve that is really a polarity?
- Which side do you lean towards by instinct – and what does that cost?

**For your team**

- Which polarity is pulling your team apart right now?
- What early warning signs would tell you that you have leaned too far?
- When did you last pause together to ask: What? So what? Now what?
